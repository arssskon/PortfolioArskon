02 — Context & Task

A customs and logistics company that had to earn trust before a single call

IMPOS TRADE (OOO, ИНН 7430017168) has operated since 2013 from Chelyabinsk offering full‑cycle foreign trade outsourcing: import and export under contract, customs clearance, international logistics, documentation, certification, and cargo insurance. Clients range from small e‑commerce importers to mid‑size manufacturers sourcing equipment from China, India, and Turkey.

In B2B customs and logistics, clients hand over large sums, foreign contracts, and legal responsibility to a third party. The website had to communicate expertise and transparency before a single conversation starts — and it had to do it in two languages simultaneously.

Import · Export · Customs China · India · Turkey RU/CN architecture 12 years in FEA
02.1 — Client

Eight services. Two languages. One website with no clear entry point.

The company offered 8 distinct service types — each for a different client with a different intent and risk profile. Import clients need a customs calculator and route options. Export clients need contract documentation. A first‑time importer needs an FAQ and step‑by‑step guide. The previous flat structure gave all of them the same undifferentiated page.

02.2 — Problem

Proof existed — scattered. Services existed — unlabeled.

The company's strongest asset — documented, real proof of results — was invisible to first‑time visitors.

03 — Process / Information Architecture

Navigation rebuilt around service type and client intent

Problem

The original structure listed all 8 services flat with no hierarchy. Import and export clients, customs clients, and China‑specific clients all landed on the same pages. The mega‑menu had no geographic grouping and no separation between service types and destination‑specific services.

Solution

Main navigation anchors five primary sections. Services expand to a mega‑menu with two sub‑tiers: by service type (import, export, FEA outsourcing, logistics, certification) and by geography (China‑specific services in a dedicated group). Content types — Cases, Reviews, News, Articles — each got standalone sections with filtering and pagination.

Navigation — mega‑menu with service type and geography sub‑tiers
04 — Process / Design System

Dark theme built from scratch — a deliberate choice for B2B trust

In the customs and logistics space, a dark interface reads as serious, professional, and trustworthy. The teal accent provides precise visual contrast and signals attention to detail. Cream headlines on dark backgrounds give warmth without sacrificing readability. All colors, typography, icons, and 20+ components were created from zero.

Design system — colors, typography, icons, components
Color

Dark background #141414, teal accent #00C4A0, cream headline #F0EDD6, dark card #1E1E1E — a four‑token palette with no ambiguity

Typography

Bold geometric sans for headlines in cream, regular sans for body in muted gray — two weights, two roles, high contrast on dark backgrounds

Components

Service cards with teal icons, filter sidebars, tabbed review blocks, delivery calculator, accordion FAQ, 4‑format review system, lead capture forms, audio and video players

05 — Process / Homepage

Homepage as the main conversion surface — 15+ sections

The homepage serves three visitor types simultaneously: first‑time importers evaluating options, existing clients checking tariffs, and Chinese trade partners looking for a Russian counterpart. The page works through a structured sequence — hero, 8‑service grid, tariff table, cases, reviews, process steps, team, and CTAs.

05.1 — Hero + Services

Above the fold: core proposition and all 8 services visible

The hero leads with "FEA Outsourcing / Import and export under contract" in large cream type on dark background. A "Calculate cost" CTA with teal button is the primary action. Directly below, all 8 service types appear as icon cards with short descriptions and pricing callouts — each linking to a dedicated service landing.

Hero + service grid — all 8 types with icons, descriptions, pricing callouts
Reviews — 4 format tabs, each presented in its native UI pattern
05.2 — Social Proof

Four formats of proof — each presented in its native style

Reviews are a key conversion driver when stakes are high. IMPOS TRADE accumulated genuine proof in four formats: messenger screenshots, official gratitude letters (including from ROSATOM), audio recordings, and video testimonials. Each format required a different UI pattern. The Reviews section presents all four as tabs on a single page — screenshot cards, document previews, audio players, video grids.

06 — Process / Inner Pages

10+ page types — each with its own conversion goal

Beyond the homepage, every page type required its own structural logic. The two most complex are shown below.

06.1 — Cases

Filterable case grid with real route and outcome data

The cases section presents 12+ published studies with real numbers: origin city, destination city, cargo weight, incoterms, delivery time, and financial outcome. A filter sidebar allows selection by geography, service type, and cargo category. Each card includes a "Download checklist" CTA for lead capture.

Cases — filterable grid, route data, real financial outcomes, checklist CTA
Service landing — calculator, advantages, FAQ, lead capture CTA
06.2 — Service Landing

Each service gets a standalone landing with calculator and FAQ

Every service type received its own page with a hero, a delivery calculator, advantages, pricing section, FAQ, and CTA. The China calculator was specified and designed in full — 16 origin countries, logistics hubs in 10 Chinese cities, and 5+ Russian delivery cities — and is pending development.

07 — Process / Responsive Design

Five breakpoints — designed in parallel, not retrofitted

Each breakpoint required specific layout decisions from the start. The mega‑menu collapses to a hamburger at 768px. The 4‑column service grid becomes 2 columns at 768px and single column at 480px. Hero text scales from 58px (1920px desktop) to 32px (320px mobile). The delivery calculator switches from horizontal to vertical card stacking.

All 5 breakpoints — 1920 · 1024 · 768 · 480 · 320px
480px — full page scroll

09 — Constraints

What shaped the decisions

01

Serving Russian‑speaking and Chinese‑speaking clients from the same domain required parallel content architecture — every form, CTA, and FAQ category needed a full Chinese‑language equivalent, not just a translation pass. The Chinese layer is designed and specified; the client has not yet deployed it.

02

Building trust for high‑stakes B2B decisions without a sales conversation — the site had to surface real proof (case studies with financial outcomes, official letters, audio and video) before any contact is initiated

03

13 page types and 40+ service landings required a complete design system before any page was drawn — components, states, and spacing tokens first

10 — Measurement Plan

What this design was built to move — and how it would be proven

Analytics access sits with the client, so post‑launch data is not mine to publish. Rather than restate scope as if it were outcome, here is what each decision was betting on and how it would be verified in the site's existing analytics.

Primary

Share of organic sessions entering on a service landing, not the homepage

The core bet of the 40‑landing architecture: 40 pages means 40 entry points from search, each matched to a specific intent. If organic traffic still lands mostly on the homepage, the architecture did not pay for itself. Verified via entry‑page segmentation.

Secondary

Conversion delta between sessions that touched a case study and those that did not

This case argues the challenge was hierarchy and access to existing proof, not inventing it. This metric tests exactly that claim — whether surfacing real financial outcomes moves intent. Verified by segmenting sessions that reached the cases section against form‑submission goals.

Guardrail

Mobile‑to‑desktop conversion parity

Five breakpoints were designed in parallel, not retrofitted. Parity between 0.8 and 1.0 confirms that claim; a steep mobile drop‑off would falsify it. Verified in the devices report against the same goal.

A metric that could only confirm the design is not a metric. Each of the three above can come back negative — that is what makes them worth naming.

11 — Reflection
"The hardest part wasn't the dark theme or the 13 page types — it was curation of proof. When a company has 12 real case studies with route data and financial outcomes, official letters from ROSATOM, and video testimonials, the design challenge is hierarchy and access — not invention. The architecture had to surface the right proof for the right visitor type before they clicked away."
